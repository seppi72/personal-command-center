# Term is an entity, not a label on a Course

A Course stored its Term as two required integers, `termMonth` and `termYear` — September 2026 — on the reasoning that a Term was just a month-and-year label with nothing of its own to hold. That reasoning has stopped being true. The owner's school runs on semesters published in an academic calendar with real start and end dates, and the owner can look those dates up. A Term now has attributes, and per-semester figures — starting with the General Weighted Average issue #92 adds — need something to hang off. A month-and-year pair is also the wrong grain for a semester: a semester spans four or five months, so "the Term a Course belongs to" and "the month written on the Course" were never the same fact.

We promoted Term to an entity. A Term is identified by its year plus its semester (1st, 2nd, or Summer), unique on that pair; its display name is derived from those two rather than stored as free text; it optionally carries the start and end dates from the school's academic calendar; and two Terms' date ranges may not overlap. `Course` gains a required reference to a Term and loses `termMonth`/`termYear`. Deleting a Term is blocked while any Course still references it, the same referential guard Course already has for its Time Entries, Personal Commitments and Projects, and Account has for its Transactions — cascading would silently delete a semester of academic history.

Three details are deliberate. The **display name is derived**, because free text would let "1st Sem 2026" and "First Semester 2026" exist as two rows with a semester's Courses split between them, breaking every per-Term figure in a way nothing would flag. The **dates are optional**, because the migration that generates Terms from existing `termMonth`/`termYear` data cannot know the school's published calendar; it leaves them null rather than fabricating a span, and a Term with no dates is never auto-selected as the current one, so "which semester is now" degrades to "no answer" instead of a confident wrong one. And **overlap is rejected on save**, because two overlapping Terms would give that same question two answers and the School screen would show a different semester depending on sort order.

The alternative — keeping Term as plain fields on Course, either the existing month-and-year or a year-plus-semester pair — was rejected because a Term now carries its own attributes, and "which semester is current" is only answerable if the school's dates live somewhere. Plain fields on Course would mean storing those dates once per Course and hoping every Course in a semester agreed about them. The cost of this decision is a broad mechanical refactor across the model, the API, the client mirror and the School screen, plus a new Terms surface for creating a Term before a Course can point at one; issue #120 carried it separately from #92 so that the rename-and-migrate diff didn't arrive tangled with new grading behavior.
